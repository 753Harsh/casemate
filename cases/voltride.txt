VoltRide: An Electric Scooter Start-up Facing a Crowded Market

VoltRide was founded in Pune in 2020 to make affordable electric scooters for college students and delivery riders. Its first model, the VoltRide S1, launched in 2021 at Rs 79,000 with a range of 90 km per charge. Thanks to a state subsidy and strong word of mouth on campuses, VoltRide sold 18,000 scooters in 2022 and reached revenue of Rs 140 crore in 2023.

The Indian electric two-wheeler market has grown quickly. Industry sales crossed 9 lakh units in 2023, up from about 2.5 lakh in 2021. But the market has become crowded. More than 40 brands now sell electric scooters, including large petrol two-wheeler makers who entered in 2022 with well-known names and dealer networks in every district. In 2023, two of these large makers cut prices by up to Rs 15,000 per scooter.

VoltRide depends on imported battery cells, which make up about 40 percent of the cost of each scooter. It buys cells from two suppliers in China. In late 2023, one supplier raised prices by 8 percent and delayed shipments by six weeks, which forced VoltRide to pause production for 18 days. The company has signed a letter of intent with an Indian cell maker, but local cells will not be available at scale before 2026.

Customers have many choices. Buyers can compare prices online in minutes, and many switch to a rival brand for a discount of as little as Rs 3,000. At the same time, petrol scooters remain a strong alternative: they cost about Rs 75,000, refuel in minutes, and have service centres everywhere. VoltRide has only 65 service points, mostly in Maharashtra and Karnataka, and 28 percent of customer complaints in 2023 were about long service waiting times.

The central government reduced the purchase subsidy in 2024, which raised the effective price of the S1 by about Rs 10,000. VoltRide's CEO, Neha Kulkarni, must now decide whether to cut prices to defend volume, launch a cheaper model with a smaller battery, or focus on the delivery-fleet segment, where companies sign contracts for hundreds of scooters at a time and care more about running cost than brand.
